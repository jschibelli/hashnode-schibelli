**How I Found AI—and Why It’s Become My Lifeline After a Stroke-Induced Brain Injury**

Life can change in an instant. One moment, you’re navigating your day just like everyone else, and in the next, everything you once took for granted is suddenly out of reach. That’s exactly what happened to me after I sustained a stroke-induced brain injury in the summer of 2022. Tasks I had performed effortlessly—writing an email, remembering appointments, or simply focusing on a book—became frustrating challenges. It felt like my brain had turned into a maze with no map. Little did I know that something as unassuming as Artificial Intelligence would turn into one of my most valuable tools for reclaiming my independence. My passion for coding was also affected, as I struggled to keep up with complex logic and recall programming concepts.

---

### My Introduction to AI

My journey with AI began in a moment of sheer desperation. I was looking for anything that could help manage my daily life. My short-term memory was unreliable, my organizational skills were chaotic at best, and I still struggle to this day to put my thoughts into words. A few months later, stumbled on by accident an article about AI so naturally I needed to know more. Unfortunately, at this stage of my recovery it was still very foggy.

I started small. At first, I used a simple voice assistant on my phone to set reminders for medication and appointments. The difference was immediate and life-changing. Suddenly, I had a helping hand that never forgot anything, even if I did. Encouraged by that success, I ventured into more advanced AI. I knew that the use of AI had to be a major part of my recovery plan. Tools that could help me break down complex tasks into smaller, more manageable steps. Apps that would generate summaries of articles or emails, saving me the mental energy of processing them in their entirety. For the first time since my injury, I felt a bit more in control.

---

### Why AI Is Vital to Me

**1\. Regaining Independence:**  
Before using AI, I had to rely heavily on friends, family, and caregivers to keep track of appointments or remind me of daily tasks. Now, AI helps me manage my schedule, make sense of large amounts of information, and store notes I can refer back to at any time. It’s like having a personal assistant who’s always on call.

**2\. Overcoming Communication Hurdles:**  
Writing and speaking coherently posed enormous hurdles for me post-injury. I have difficulty writing because my sentences come out garbled. I would often stutter when I wrote something, repeating the same words, parts of sentences, or whole sentences written a little differently from the original. Often, my thoughts raced too quickly for me to organize them clearly. AI tools helped refine my writing, turning scattered ideas into readable text. I used blogging as a therapy mechanism, allowing me to stay current in my profession and learn new and exciting technologies.

**3\. Boosting Confidence and Mental Energy:**  
One of the most frustrating aspects of dealing with a brain injury is the way it wears down your self-esteem. You start doubting your capabilities—something as simple as forgetting a phone number can feel devastating. AI cuts through that fog of uncertainty. It provides a backup for my memory, and it supports my cognitive gaps, freeing up mental space so I can focus on healing and enjoying life rather than fretting over every little detail.

**4\. Reclaiming My Coding Career:** Another reason AI is vital for me is the essential role it plays in helping me relearn and retain complex coding concepts. My stroke-induced brain injury forced me to step away from programming, but AI has become my ally in bridging the gaps in my cognitive abilities. Tools like code generation, syntax checkers, and debugging assistants help me structure my thoughts, correct mistakes, and maintain focus. Over time, these tools have allowed me to gradually rebuild my confidence and skills, bringing me closer to resuming the coding career I’m passionate about. In April of 2024, I officially resumed my coding career. Post-injury, I became much more efficient and have regained almost 100% of my coding ability by developing a system that helps me code and retain new concepts. I have now expanded my skills into Machine Learning, allowing me to leverage AI even further in my development journey.

---

### Comparing Life With and Without AI

If I had never discovered AI, I’d still be grappling with the day-to-day chaos of juggling tasks in my head, constantly second-guessing whether I’d remembered to do something. My reliance on other people would be significantly higher, and the strain on my emotional health would be immense.

Without AI:

* I’d spend hours every day trying to organize my thoughts, often without success.
    
* I would have never been able to resume my career as a software engineer.
    
* My stress levels would be through the roof because of missed appointments and forgotten tasks.
    
* I’d risk missing critical medication times, further impacting my health and recovery.
    
* Communicating—especially through writing—would be a painfully slow process, often leading me to give up.
    

With AI:

* I have structured routines for each day, complete with automated reminders that I trust.
    
* I am now a Tech writer on multiple platforms, sharing my knowledge and experiences with a wider audience.
    
* I have a sense of control over my life; the unpredictability is still there, but it’s more manageable.
    
* I can share my story through writing (like this blog post) in a way I never thought possible after my injury.
    
* My passion and career can advance since I am in the best position as a software engineer to get the best out of AI.
    

---

### Moving Forward With Hope

Of course, AI isn’t a magical cure-all. I still have tough days and ongoing challenges related to my injury. But these tools offer a lifeline—a way to shore up the parts of my mind that still need time to heal. Every time AI helps me complete a task, it’s a small victory that boosts my confidence and lets me feel a little more like myself again. It has help re-train my brain so I can now move forward, reclaim what I once lost.

I often think about how far technology has come. As a Gen X’er, we taught ourselves to be resourceful. We of course are the last generation before the internet. It wasn’t too long ago, the idea of having a digital companion who can understand natural language and provide real-time assistance sounded like science fiction. Now it’s part of my daily routine, and I am incredibly grateful. If you’re in a similar situation—whether you’ve faced a serious injury or are simply looking to streamline your life—AI might just change everything for you too.

In sharing my story, I hope to offer a bit of inspiration. Brain injuries can be isolating and intimidating, but they don’t have to strip you of hope. For me, AI is more than just a tool; it’s the bridge between where I am and where I want to be. I may not fully control what happened to me, but I can use every resource available to continue moving forward, one AI-assisted step at a time.
